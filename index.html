About About me

Research grounded in how machines see and decide

I am a Ph.D. Researcher in Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning at Vellore Institute of Technology, specialising in Autonomous Systems, and an Assistant Professor (Junior) at VIT.

I am interested in intelligent systems that perceive, learn, and make decisions in complex environments. Much of my work sits where computer vision meets language: helping models describe what they see in a driving scene, and making that reasoning open to inspection.

I care about the step from idea to working system. Before my doctoral research I built segmentation models for drone-based infrastructure inspection, a gesture recognition system for American Sign Language, and an internal ticketing portal for a national research institute.

Current research direction

Explainable Vision–Language Models for Autonomous Driving Systems

My research explores how vision–language models can understand challenging driving scenes and provide explanations grounded in both visual evidence and language. I am particularly interested in contextual reasoning, faithful explanations, and evaluating model behaviour in complex and edge-case scenarios.

Driving Scene Understanding

Driving scenes are rarely tidy. A stalled truck, a closed lane and a cyclist moving into the gap only make sense when read together. I study how vision–language models reason over that context, especially in unusual and edge-case situations.
